For twenty years, "getting found online" meant one thing: ranking on Google. You wrote for a search box and a human who'd scan ten blue links.

That era is ending. More and more, the thing doing the searching isn't a person — it's an AI agent answering a question on someone's behalf. Someone asks ChatGPT "who can help me implement AI in my coaching business?" and the model goes and reads the web to answer. If your site isn't legible to that agent, you simply don't exist in the answer.

## Agents read differently than people

A human skims. They forgive messy layouts, infer your offer from a vibe, click around. An AI agent does none of that. It wants:

- **Clear, factual statements** about who you are and what you do.
- **Structured data** it can parse without guessing.
- **Plain summaries** of each page, not just marketing flourishes.
- A **machine-readable map** of your site.

If your content is locked inside images, animations, or JavaScript an agent can't run, it's invisible — no matter how beautiful it looks to a human.

## Three things every page should have

You don't need to choose between writing for humans and writing for agents. You need both layers on the same page.

1. **A one-sentence answer to "what is this page about?"** Put it near the top, in plain words. Agents lift these almost verbatim.
2. **Structured metadata.** Schema.org markup tells an agent "this is a person, this is a service, this is an article, written on this date." It removes the guesswork.
3. **A summary it can quote.** A short, factual paragraph an agent can pull into its answer without rewording.

> The goal isn't to trick the algorithm. It's to be unmistakably clear about who you help and how — so clear that a machine can repeat it accurately.

## The new file agents look for

There's an emerging convention called `llms.txt` — a plain-text file at the root of your site that tells AI agents, in clean prose, what your business is and where the important pages live. Think of it as a cover letter written for machines. It's early, but the sites that adopt it now are the ones agents will describe most accurately later.

*(This site has one — you can read it at [/llms.txt](/llms.txt).)*

## Write the answer, not just the ad

The shift is subtle but real: stop writing only to persuade, and start also writing to **inform a machine that's deciding whether to recommend you.** Say plainly what you do, who it's for, and why it's different. Back it with structure. Keep it current.

The businesses that win the next decade of search won't be the loudest. They'll be the clearest.
